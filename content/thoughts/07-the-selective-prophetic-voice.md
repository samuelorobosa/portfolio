---
title: The Selective Prophetic Voice
kind: thought
---
When a man steps into public moral discourse voluntarily, he is not just offering an opinion. He is setting a standard. And that standard will be used to measure him, not by his enemies, but by the logic of his own choices.

This is why consistency matters more than almost anything else for public voices, especially religious ones. The moment you speak, you create a precedent. The moment you go silent in a situation that mirrors the ones that moved you to speak before, that silence becomes a statement. It does not exist in a vacuum. It exists in contrast.

There is a kind of selective prophetic voice that does more damage than no voice at all. A clergyman who never comments on politics is at least consistent. You know where he stands and why. But a clergyman who speaks when it suits him and goes quiet when it does not has revealed something important: his interventions were never really about principle. They were about preference. And once that becomes clear, every past statement gets retroactively reframed.
